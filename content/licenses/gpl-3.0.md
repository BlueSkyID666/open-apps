## What the GPL-3.0 lets you do

The GNU General Public License version 3 is a copyleft licence. You may use, study, modify and share the software for any purpose, including commercial use — running a GPL app inside your company, for example, carries no extra obligations.

## When you must share source code

The obligations start when you **distribute** the software or a modified version to someone else — selling it, bundling it in a product, publishing a build. Then you must:

- license the whole work, including your changes, under GPL-3.0;
- provide the complete corresponding source code, or a written offer to do so;
- keep copyright and licence notices intact;
- not impose further restrictions on the rights the GPL gives the people who receive it.

Using a GPL app over a network without distributing it does not trigger these duties; that gap is what [AGPL-3.0](/licenses/agpl-3.0/) closes.

## Can I use GPL-3.0 code in a closed-source product?

Not if you distribute that product. Linking GPL code into your program generally makes the combined work subject to the GPL. If your business depends on keeping code closed, look at [MIT](/licenses/mit/) or [Apache-2.0](/licenses/apache-2.0/) projects, or ask the authors about a commercial licence — some GPL projects offer one.

## GPL-3.0 compared with other common licences

| | [MIT](/licenses/mit/) | [Apache-2.0](/licenses/apache-2.0/) | GPL-3.0 | [AGPL-3.0](/licenses/agpl-3.0/) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Use internally, unchanged or modified | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Distribute inside a closed-source product | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Offer as a hosted service without sharing changes | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |
| Explicit patent grant | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
