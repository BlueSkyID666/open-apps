## What the Apache License 2.0 lets you do

Apache-2.0 is a permissive open-source licence. Like MIT, it lets you use, modify and distribute the software — including inside commercial, closed-source products — without publishing your own code.

## What you must keep

When you redistribute the software or a modified version you must:

- include a copy of the licence;
- keep existing copyright, patent, trademark and attribution notices;
- carry forward the project's `NOTICE` file, if it has one;
- mark files you changed as modified.

## The patent grant

What sets Apache-2.0 apart from MIT is an explicit patent licence: every contributor grants users a licence to any of their patents that their contribution necessarily uses. If you start patent litigation claiming the software infringes a patent, the patent licences granted to you under Apache-2.0 end. This makes Apache-2.0 a common choice for company-backed projects.

## Can I use Apache-2.0 code in a commercial app?

Yes, with the notices above. You may license your own changes however you like. The licence does not grant rights to the project's trademarks, so do not ship your product under the original project's name or logo.

## Apache-2.0 compared with other common licences

| | [MIT](/licenses/mit/) | Apache-2.0 | [GPL-3.0](/licenses/gpl-3.0/) | [AGPL-3.0](/licenses/agpl-3.0/) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Use in closed-source products | Yes | Yes | Only if you do not distribute it | Only if you neither distribute it nor offer it over a network |
| Explicit patent grant | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| NOTICE file and change marking | No | Yes | — | — |
| Must share your changes | No | No | When you distribute | When you distribute or serve it over a network |

Apache-2.0 code can be combined into GPL-3.0 projects; the reverse is not allowed.
